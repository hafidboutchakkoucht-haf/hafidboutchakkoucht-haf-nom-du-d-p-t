Guides

The essentials, jargon-free

Five short guides written from our field experience.

Erasmus+ in plain words

Erasmus+ is the European Union’s programme for education, training, youth and sport. The current programme runs from 2021 to 2027. It is not only for students: its Youth strand funds projects run by NGOs and open to all young people.

Its four priorities: inclusion and diversity, digital transformation, environment and the fight against climate change, participation in democratic life.

  • Youth exchanges: groups of young people aged 13 to 30, from at least two countries, meet for 5 to 21 days around a theme.
  • Mobility of youth workers: training courses, seminars and study visits for those who support young people.
  • Cooperation partnerships: organisations from several countries work together over time.

In France, the Youth strand is managed by the Agence Erasmus+ France Jeunesse & Sport.

What does a day on an exchange look like?

A typical day starts around 9 am with an “energizer”, a short game to wake the group up. The morning is for workshops: case studies, small-group work, debates. The afternoon often goes out into the field, meeting local businesses, farms or NGOs. The day ends with a group debrief.

Evenings matter as much as workshops: at the intercultural evening, each country shares its food, music and traditions.

The method is called non-formal learning: no lectures, no grades, but clear learning objectives and a supportive setting.

Youthpass: getting your learning recognised

Youthpass is the European tool for recognising non-formal learning. It is issued at the end of an Erasmus+ Youth or European Solidarity Corps project.

It builds on the eight European key competences, including multilingual, digital, citizenship and entrepreneurship competences, as well as “learning to learn”.

Our tip: each evening, write down one thing you learned. On the last day, filling in your Youthpass will take ten minutes, and you will have precise examples to quote in an interview.

Travel and reimbursement: how it works

Erasmus+ contributes to travel costs according to the distance between your starting point and the project venue. The ceiling for your country is stated in the infopack.

  1. Wait for confirmation of your place before buying tickets.
  2. Choose train, bus or carpooling where you can: the programme encourages green travel.
  3. Keep everything: original invoices, tickets, boarding passes.
  4. Take part in all activities.
  5. Complete your final participant report: reimbursement comes after the project.
Setting up a project: where to start?

Are you an NGO wanting to run your own exchange?

  1. Register your organisation to obtain an OID.
  2. Find partners: SALTO-YOUTH’s OTLAS platform is built for that.
  3. Start from a real need of your young people and your area, not a fashionable theme.
  4. Read the current Programme Guide: criteria, deadlines and amounts are all there.
  5. Contact your National Agency, which supports applicants.

Glossary

The vocabulary of mobility

Erasmus+
The EU programme for education, training, youth and sport (2021–2027).
Youth exchange
A meeting of groups of young people from several countries, lasting 5 to 21 days, around a shared theme.
Youthpass
European certificate recognising competences gained in a youth project.
Non-formal learning
Voluntary, structured learning outside the school system, based on practice and group work.
Young people with fewer opportunities
Young people facing social, economic, geographical, health or disability-related obstacles that limit their access to mobility.
Sending organisation
The organisation in your country that selects, prepares and supports you.
Hosting organisation
The organisation running the project on site: programme, accommodation, meals.
Group leader
An adult accompanying a national group during the exchange. No age limit.
Infopack
Document sent before departure: programme, venue, directions, reimbursement rules, packing list.
Green travel
Travel mainly by train, bus or carpooling, encouraged by Erasmus+.
Energizer
A short, lively game to kick off a session and bond the group.
Intercultural evening
A highlight where each country presents its food, music and traditions.
National Agency
The body managing Erasmus+ in each country and selecting funded projects.
OID
Organisation ID, required to apply for or join an Erasmus+ project.
Dissemination
Sharing what the project produced and taught with your community and networks.
European Solidarity Corps
EU programme for volunteering and solidarity projects for 18–30 year-olds.

FAQ

Your questions, our answers

Is a question missing? Ask us.

How much does a youth exchange cost?

In our Erasmus+ exchanges, accommodation, three meals a day and activities are covered. Travel is reimbursed after the project, up to the limits set by Erasmus+. Personal expenses and the food you bring for the intercultural evening are at your own cost.

Do I need good English?

No. English is the working language, but all levels are welcome and the group helps each other. You can also use other languages during intercultural moments.

Who can take part?

Our exchanges are for young people aged 18 to 30. Group leaders have no age limit. Each partner organisation includes young people with fewer opportunities and ensures a balanced group.

How is travel reimbursed?

After the project, on presentation of original invoices, boarding passes or tickets, and once you have completed your final participant report. Unjustified absence from workshops may mean no reimbursement.

What is Youthpass?

It is the European certificate recognising what you learned in an Erasmus+ Youth or European Solidarity Corps project. It describes what you did and the competences you developed; you can attach it to a CV.

I have a special diet or medical treatment

State it in the registration form. Diets and allergies are catered for, and the team needs to know about any treatment.

Can my organisation become a partner?

Yes. Write to us introducing your organisation, your target group and your idea. To take part in Erasmus+, an organisation must be registered with the European Commission and hold an OID.

Ready to cross the border?

Young person, youth worker, NGO or local authority: write to us. We answer every message and point you to the opportunity that fits you.
